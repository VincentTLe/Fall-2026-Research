# Summary of the reconstruction experiments for the Nov 3 meeting (P4), 2026-10-29

## 1. Result

Two of the four questions now have real numbers. On how many cameras we need (Q1), 5–6 cameras spread around the
person come within about 17 mm of the reference, and bunching them on one side costs as much as dropping to 3. On one
camera vs. two for rehab exercises (Q2), two calibrated cameras get to 42.9 mm without any alignment. One camera
(GVHMR) is at 46–70 mm even after the best possible alignment. GVHMR also beats every method in the REHAB24-6
authors' own benchmark: 6.00 cm, against their best of 8.4 cm. The avatar (Q3) works, and its limit is pose accuracy
rather than appearance. Internet video (Q4) has not been started. Q2 looks like the best candidate for a paper,
because it tests a published recommendation (use one 2D camera) with ground truth. But the claim it needs most, two
cameras plus a body model, has not been run yet.

Metrics, one line each:
- **MPJPE:** average distance between estimated and true 3D joints.
- **PA-MPJPE:** the same, after the best rotation, shift and scale, so it measures only the shape of the pose.

  lower LPIPS is better.

## 2. The four questions

### Q1. Cameras vs. accuracy on real data

**What the results show.** Conditions: CMU Panoptic `171204_pose1`, 392 frames of one person doing a slow
range-of-motion exercise. EasyMocap `e6006fd` triangulation with HRNet 2D, no SMPL. The 10 limb joints are scored
against the dome's own 3D skeletons. That is a dense multi-camera reconstruction, **not motion capture** [T1].

| Cameras | MPJPE, 10 limb joints | Note |
|---|---|---|
| All 31 | 11.7 mm | [T1] |
| 6 spread (mean of 3 sets) | 16.8 mm | [T1] |
| 5 spread (mean of 3 sets) | 16.8 mm | [T1] |
| 4 spread (mean of 3 sets) | 19.0 mm | [T1] |
| 3 spread (mean of 3 sets) | 23.0 mm | optimistic: 2 of the 3 sets lost 23–28% of the limb joints [T1] |
| 5 bunched in a ~90–100° arc | 24.6–26.0 mm | [T1] |

- **Most of the gain comes between 3 and 5 cameras, and 6 adds nothing on average.** The simulation predicted the same
  shape: 3 → 4 → 5 → 6 cameras gave 11.7 → 10.9 → 8.9 → 8.1 mm on synthetic data [SYN].
- **Layout matters much more on real data than the simulation predicted.** In simulation, 5 cameras in a 90° cluster
  were only about 10% worse (9.8 vs. 8.9 mm) [SYN]. On Panoptic, a bunched rig is as bad as 3 spread cameras [T1].
  The simulation file itself warned against concluding "front-only is fine" [SYN].
- **Sync is forgiving for slow motion.** Shifting one of 5 cameras by 1, 2, 4 or 8 frames (33–267 ms) adds +0.3, +0.9,
  +1.8 and +2.3 mm [T1]. The proposed rig target, residual offset ≤ 1 frame at 60 fps, still holds [SYN, T1].
- **Feet:** the ViTPose+ detector gives foot points through EasyMocap without code changes. Its body joints are
  5.5 mm worse than HRNet's on Panoptic. HRNet body + ViTPose feet gives HRNet's body plus feet, and visibly better
  feet on the SMPL mesh, judged by eye on 2 frames × 2 cameras [T1].
- **The ZJU reprojection tail is explained.** Full `zju-ls-feng` run (23 cameras, 800 frames): median 5.55 px, p95
  21.4 px. The tail comes from low-confidence 2D points (56% of it) and from wrists and elbows, not from a badly
  calibrated camera [T1].

**What it does not show yet.**
- Only one sequence and one subject, a 13 s slow clip [T1].
- The reference is the dome, not mocap.
- There is no SMPL accuracy anywhere: the body model was not scored on Panoptic.
- Only one camera height ring was tried, and no fast motion.
- No two cameras were shifted at once.

### Q2. One camera vs. a few cameras for rehab motion

**What prior work already showed** (read in `~/fall2026/data/papers/`; numbers only where a result file quotes them):
- *Information Systems 2025* (REHAB24-6 benchmark, Table 5). Single-camera 3D methods on REHAB24-6 against mocap. The
  best is MediaPipe Pose full, at BestFit 8.4 cm / BBox 11.7 cm. Even MotionBERT-lite given ideal ground-truth 2D input
  is at 7.7 cm [T2b quotes Table 5; I checked those five rows against the paper]. The authors conclude that depth is
  the main problem and recommend 2D estimators over 3D where possible. Their methods are MediaPipe, MotionBERT,
  MHFormer and STCFormer. There is no body-model method like GVHMR and no multi-camera result.
- *Jánošová et al., arXiv 2609.35726* (REHAB26-ViewAngles). This paper introduces Ψ. With MediaPipe, 2D separates
  correct from incorrect repetitions better than 3D, most clearly for the squat. Of their two-camera strategies,
  combining two orthogonal 2D views works best, while triangulation is less consistent. T5 quotes their best
  multi-camera rows as Ortho. 0.58–0.76. That range is the arm exercises; their squat row is lower. They recommend a
  single 2D camera as the main approach, because two cameras need calibration and sync [T5 §1, §8; paper Sec. 5.3, 6].
- Neither paper scores one camera against two cameras **in millimetres against mocap**, and neither uses a modern
  body-model method.

**What our results add, each with its caveats.**

1. **One vs. two cameras, in mm, on the same frames.** Conditions: REHAB24-6, 12 of 65 recordings (7 of the 10
   subjects; lights on and off), the 183 repetitions that are clean in both cameras (18,584 frames), 10 limb joints
   [T2, T2b].

   | Method | No alignment (abs) | PA-MPJPE |
   |---|---|---|
   | Two cameras, ViTPose 2D triangulated, authors' **new** cameras | **42.9 mm** | 37.0 mm [T2b] |
   | Two cameras, same, **old** cameras (= the dataset's own camera model) | 47.5 mm | 36.9 mm [T2] |
   | Two cameras, EasyMocap HRNet, new / old cameras | 53.3 / 57.2 mm | 43.1 mm [T2b] |
   | GVHMR, horizontal camera 17 alone | 182–197 mm | 46.8 mm [T2, T2b] |
   | GVHMR, vertical camera 18 alone | 160–205 mm | 69.6 mm [T2, T2b] |

   - Two cameras need no alignment and are still as good as or better than one camera **after** a per-frame
     best-fit alignment.
   - The main one-camera failure is a far-side arm hidden behind the body in profile view. Camera 18 in profile
     scores PA 89.9 mm [T2]. Wrists are the worst joints, at 61–132 mm PA [T2].
   - Caveats:
     - GVHMR weights come from an **unofficial mirror** [T2].
     - The two-camera result is triangulated joints, **not a body model**.
     - Only 12 recordings and 7 subjects; lighting is confounded with subject [T2].
     - The new cameras came privately by email from the dataset authors [T2b].

2. **GVHMR on the authors' own benchmark.** Conditions: all 65 recordings, both cameras, 256,892 frames, the paper's
   12 joints [T2b].
   - GVHMR scores **BestFit 6.00 cm** (7.16 cm if fitted once per repetition) and BBox 7.94 cm.
   - The paper's best is 8.4 / 11.7 cm, and its ideal-2D result is 7.7 / 11.3 cm.
   - GVHMR is lower in 21 of 22 exercise × view cells. The exception is Ex4 horizontal front: 5.97 vs. 5.5 cm.
   - Caveats:
     - **Unofficial weights.**
     - The paper leaves three protocol details unstated: BestFit granularity, IoU threshold and BBox formula. BBox is
       our reconstruction, so treat it as approximate [T2b].
     - GVHMR is a newer method than the ones in the paper.
     - Healthy volunteers, not patients.

3. **Exercise assessment (Ψ).** Conditions: REHAB26-ViewAngles, all 495 sequences, **5 people**, 95% intervals
   resampled over persons [T5].
   - **Arm abduction:** GVHMR beats MediaPipe 2D by +0.112 [+0.094, +0.131].
   - **Shoulder extension:** GVHMR beats MediaPipe 2D by +0.091 [+0.055, +0.124]. Most of this gain comes from
     GVHMR's better 2D detector. GVHMR minus its own ViTPose 2D is +0.028 [−0.038, +0.083].
   - **Squat: not settled.** At the default settings GVHMR is ahead by +0.042. On the 240 held-out sequences it is
     +0.017, with an interval that includes 0. If 3D angles are measured in full 3D, it flips to −0.090.
   - **Against MediaPipe 3D**, GVHMR is better on all three exercises: +0.142, +0.136 and +0.093.
   - Caveats:
     - The paper's baseline was reproduced **only to grade B**: 80 of 90 cells within 20%, correlation 0.69.
     - **Unofficial weights**, and GVHMR's default focal length.
     - **5 people.**
     - Ψ's noise floor is about 0.1, not 0 [T5].
   - So, with GVHMR, "2D beats monocular 3D" does not hold for the two arm exercises. That is a claim against a
     published result, so the caveats matter.

4. **The dataset's camera model.**
   - REHAB24-6 ships no calibration. We recovered it from the 2D/3D joints, and it equals the authors' "old" cameras
     exactly (0.0000 mm, 0.00000°) [T2b].
   - The authors' newer cameras fit the real video better. Median joint distance to ViTPose drops from 13.5 to
     11.5 px on camera 17 and from 24.9 to 21.3 px on camera 18. Two-camera error improves on every exercise [T2b].
   - About 2 px (5–10%) of the paper's reported 2D error comes from the rough calibration [T2b].

**What it does not show yet.**
- Two cameras **plus a body model** (SMPL), scored on the same frames and on the paper's protocol. Telepresence needs
  that, and it is the fair "multi-view + SMPL" side of Q2.
- Two-camera exercise assessment with GVHMR or ViTPose, to set against the paper's Ortho. rows.
- Official GVHMR weights.
- The other 53 recordings for the mm comparison.
- Patients.

### Q3. Avatar quality from cheap input

**What the results show** (GaussianAvatar stage 1, RTX A6000) [T4]:
- **The method reproduces.**
  - The authors' checkpoint gives 28.83 / 0.9759 / 0.0230 (PSNR / SSIM / LPIPS) on People-Snapshot m4c, against the
    paper's 28.78 / 0.9755 / 0.0228.
  - Our own 1000-epoch training gives **28.80 / 0.9760 / 0.0225**. The README's 200-epoch command gives only 28.57,
    because it trains about 5× shorter than the released checkpoint.
  - The avatar renders at about 38 fps, so it is real-time.
- **Phone video (NeuMan bike):**
  - With the given test poses: 23.92 / 0.9600 / 0.0267.
  - With test poses refined against the test images: 29.43 / 0.9767 / 0.0151. That is optimistic by construction.
  - The paper's 4-sequence average is 29.94, and its protocol is not described.
- **Our kind of data** (REHAB24-6 PM_037, camera 17 only; GVHMR poses; SAM 2.1 masks; 354 training frames; 2 h
  training, 21 GB):
  - On 3 held-out repetitions: **18.54 / 0.9430 / 0.0663** with raw GVHMR poses, and 28.06 / 0.9778 / 0.0197 with
    refined poses (diagnostic, optimistic).
  - The 9.5 dB gap is pose error, not colour error. Silhouette overlap with the real person is 0.78–0.85 with raw
    poses and 0.97 with refined ones.
  - Training had to move the training poses by about 10 cm, mostly in depth, and differently in every frame.
  - The body holds together through a 360° turn. Weak spots: suit markers blur to dots, the never-seen inner legs
    show a faint grid, and the face is soft from the side.
- **Static scene:** the nerfstudio toolchain works, at PSNR 35.09 on the `poster` sample. That sample has only 100 of
  its 226 frames [T4].

**What it does not show yet.**
- **How quality changes with the number of input views.** That is half of Q3, and only one camera has been used.
- Our own phone or GoPro footage.
- A fair way to set the test poses: both refined rows fit on the test images themselves.
- Run-to-run variation: there is one training run per setting.

## 5. Slide outline (7 slides, about 10 minutes)

### Slide 1. What we asked and what we tried (1 min)
- The question: how well can a few ordinary cameras measure a person's 3D motion, for rehab checks and avatars?
- Four questions:
  - **Q1** how many cameras;
  - **Q2** one camera or two for rehab;
  - **Q3** avatar from cheap video;
  - **Q4** internet video.
- What was tried:
  - EasyMocap multi-camera triangulation on CMU Panoptic;
  - GVHMR (a recent one-camera body-model method) on REHAB24-6, which has motion capture, and on REHAB26;
  - GaussianAvatar for avatars.
- Everything runs on public data with ground truth, on the school GPU server.
- Metric box, one line each:
  - **MPJPE** = average 3D joint distance to the truth;
  - **PA-MPJPE** = the same after the best rotation, shift and scale, so pose shape only;
  - **Ψ** = how well correct and incorrect repetitions are told apart (0.1 is chance);
  - **PSNR / SSIM / LPIPS** = how close the rendered avatar image is to the real photo.

### Slide 2. How many cameras? (1.5 min)
- Figure: `reconstruction/results/figures/q1_camera_count.png`.
- Table: all 31 cameras 11.7 mm; 6 spread 16.8; 5 spread 16.8; 4 spread 19.0; 3 spread 23.0 (and 23–28% of joints lost
  in 2 of 3 sets); 5 bunched 24.6–26.0 mm [T1].
- Takeaways:
  - 5 spread cameras is the sweet spot;
  - where the cameras stand matters as much as how many there are;
  - one frame out of sync adds only 0.3 mm.
- Caveat: one person, one slow clip, scored against the dome's reconstruction rather than mocap.

### Slide 3. One camera vs. two cameras for rehab (2 min)
- Figure: `reconstruction/results/figures/q2_one_vs_two_cameras.png`.
- Same 18,584 frames, REHAB24-6, against motion capture [T2, T2b]:
  - two cameras: **42.9 mm with no alignment at all**;
  - one camera (GVHMR): 46.8 / 69.6 mm, and that is *after* the best possible alignment;
  - one camera without alignment: about 160–205 mm off in where the body is.
- Why one camera fails: in side view the arm behind the body is hidden and gets guessed wrong. Wrists are worst.
- Caveats: 12 recordings, 7 people; unofficial GVHMR weights; two-camera result is joints, not yet a body model.

### Slide 4. Prior work and what is new (2 min)
- Figures: `reconstruction/results/figures/q2c_rehab24_benchmark.png`, `q2b_assessment_separability.png`.
- **Already known** (same Masaryk group):
  - the REHAB24-6 benchmark, whose best is MediaPipe at 8.4 cm (7.7 cm even with perfect 2D input);
  - the 2026 arXiv study, which found 2D beats 3D for judging exercises and recommends one 2D camera.
- **New from us:**
  - GVHMR on their benchmark: **6.00 cm**, lower in 21 of 22 cells [T2b];
  - for judging exercises, GVHMR beats 2D on both arm exercises (Ψ +0.11, +0.09); the squat is undecided [T5];
  - one vs. two cameras measured in mm (slide 3);
  - their camera model checked: the newer one fits the video better [T2b].
- Caveats: unofficial weights; three benchmark details not stated in the paper; their Ψ reproduced only
  approximately (grade B); 5 people in REHAB26.

### Slide 5. An avatar from one camera (1.5 min)
- Picture (server only, never in git): `~/fall2026/runs/p3-ga-rehab/stage1/novel_pose_turntable/ours_200/00060.png`.
  It is the back view of the 360° turntable, with no face visible (checked). The full turntable is
  `~/fall2026/runs/p3-ga-rehab/stage1/rehab_turntable_epoch200.mp4`, which shows the face at some angles; use only in

- The method reproduces the authors' result: 28.80 vs. 28.83 dB, rendering at 38 fps [T4].
- On REHAB24-6, one camera and 2 h of training: 18.5 dB with GVHMR's poses on new repetitions, 28.1 dB once the poses
  are corrected [T4].
- Takeaway: the avatar looks right; the bottleneck is pose accuracy, which links back to Q2.

### Slide 6. Problems (1 min)
- GVHMR weights come from an unofficial mirror, because the official download was blocked by a quota.
- Published protocols leave choices unstated, so our comparisons with the papers have readings, not one exact
  number.
- Small person counts: REHAB26 has 5 people. All subjects are healthy volunteers.
- Panoptic is one clip, and its reference is not mocap.
- Q3 has one camera so far, and Q4 is not started (needs clip URLs).
- Server issues, all handled: an out-of-memory kill, a reboot, jobs on the wrong GPU.

### Slide 7. Which question is worth a paper, and next steps (1 min)
- **Q2: one camera vs. two cameras for rehab exercise assessment, with a modern body-model method.**
- Why Q2:
  - it has motion-capture ground truth;
  - it tests a published recommendation (one 2D camera) and already disagrees with part of it;
  - the dataset authors are engaged (they sent their cameras);
  - the missing pieces reuse outputs we already have.
- Next, in order:
  1. two cameras + SMPL, scored in mm and on the benchmark;
  2. two-camera assessment (Ψ);
  3. official GVHMR weights;
  4. the authors' evaluation details.
- Honest limit: we have read only the two papers from this group. A wider literature search is needed before calling
  anything "first".
- Q1 mostly settles our own rig design (5 spread cameras, sync within 1 frame); Q3 depends on Q2's poses.
