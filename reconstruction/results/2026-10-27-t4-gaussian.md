## 1. Result

The nerfstudio + gsplat + COLMAP toolchain works on the server. There was no phone video, so (per Tan) Step 1 used
nerfstudio's sample capture `poster`. COLMAP posed **98 of 100** frames. splatfacto trained 30k steps in about 10 min
using under 2 GB of GPU memory, and reaches **PSNR 35.1 / SSIM 0.967 / LPIPS 0.079** on 9 held-out frames. The
original download link for `poster` is dead. The copy used here (nerfstudio team's Hugging Face) has only 100 of the
capture's 226 frames. GaussianAvatar (Steps 2–3) is blocked: its data is in a OneDrive folder that needs a browser,
and building its CUDA code was not permitted in this session.

## 4. Numbers

**COLMAP** (`poster`, 100 frames 1080×1920, SIFT on GPU 0, exhaustive matching): mean 3730 features per image
(min 1122). 1509 image pairs had matches. The mapper built two models:

| Model | Registered images | 3D points | Mean reprojection error |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sparse/0` | 2 (frames #73, #76, a weak first pair) | 300 | 0.17 px |
| `sparse/1` | **98** | 12,651 | 0.82 px |

The two models share no frames (2 + 98 = 100). Focal length from `sparse/1`: fx 1145.7, fy 1156.2 px. The capture's
own transforms.json gives fx 1147.2, fy 1158.8, so they agree within 0.2%. COLMAP steps took about 1 min in total.

**splatfacto** (nerfstudio 1.1.5 defaults, 30,000 steps, auto downscale ×2 → 540×960, `fraction` split 0.9 → 89
train / 9 eval frames, GPU 0):

Two build problems, each fixed once:

1. **The system gcc is too new.** Ubuntu's gcc 13.3 fails nvcc 11.8's check ("gcc versions later than 11 are not
   supported"). Fix: conda-forge `gcc_linux-64=11` / `gxx_linux-64=11` in the env, with `CC`/`CXX` set to them.
2. **The current 3DGS rasterizer has a different API.** 3DGS `main` pins diff-gaussian-rasterization on the `dr_aa`
   branch (`9c5c202`, 2024-09). It adds a required `antialiasing` field and returns `(color, radii, invdepths)`.
   GaussianAvatar's `gaussian_renderer/__init__.py` passes no `antialiasing` and unpacks `rendered_image, _`. It would
   fail at its first render. Fix: pin 3DGS to `b17ded9` (above), whose rasterizer returns `color, radii`.

Tiny test (`CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0`, script kept outside the repo; output in the log):
- Rasterizer, 128×128, 60° FOV, three Gaussians. The one straight ahead at z = 3 gives center pixel RGB (0.893, 0, 0)
  for opacity 0.9. The one at x = 1, z = 3 peaks at column 100; the pinhole model predicts 100.5. The one behind the
  camera has radius 0 and contributes no color. The backward pass gives finite gradients on the 2 visible means.
- simple-knn `distCUDA2` on 2000 random points vs. brute force on the CPU: max relative error 1.6e-7.
- pytorch3d `knn_points` on CUDA vs. `torch.cdist`: max abs error 3.8e-7. `axis_angle_to_matrix` (z, 90°) gives the
  expected matrix.
- `import arguments, gaussian_renderer, model.avatar_model, scene` from the GaussianAvatar repo: OK.
